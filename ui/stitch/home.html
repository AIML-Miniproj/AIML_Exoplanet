Astrophysical Foundations

WORLDS IN ORBIT AROUND OTHER SUNS

An exoplanet is any planet beyond our Solar System. Because stars are blindingly bright and millions of times larger than planets, directly photographing them is near impossible.

Instead, NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) stares at thousands of stars simultaneously, capturing the faint periodic dips in starlight created when an orbiting world crosses the stellar face.

Observation Method

THE TRANSIT PHOTOMETRY PRINCIPLE

When an exoplanet’s orbital plane aligns with our line of sight, it temporarily blocks a fraction of its star’s radiant flux.

Illustration · planet crossing a Sun-like star
A transit lasts a few hours

01. Alignment

Planet crosses stellar disk directly between host star and TESS sensor array.

02. Flux Attenuation

Starlight drops by about 0.01% to a few percent, depending on the planet's size.

03. U-Shape Bottom

A flat-bottomed dip means a dark body fully in front of the star; two grazing stars give a V.

04. Periodic Cadence

Dips at a fixed interval give the orbital period; the BLS search finds it across sectors.

Signal Classification Taxonomy

THE FOUR STELLAR PROFILES

Light curve signals fall into four major astrophysical archetypes. Learning their distinctive morphological signatures is essential to separating real worlds from cosmic noise.

Planet Type A
Flat-Bottom U-Dip

Confirmed Exoplanet

Sharp, periodic, flat-bottomed ingress and egress dips. Symmetric occultation caused by an opaque spherical companion.

Dip Depth: ~0.01% - 3%
Binary (EB) Type B
Alternating V-Dips

Eclipsing Binary

Two orbiting stars mutual-occulting. Features distinct primary deep dips and shallow secondary eclipses with V-shaped curves.

Dip Depth: ~1% - 50%
Variable (V) Type C
Sinusoidal Pulsation

Pulsating Variable

Continuous smooth expansion and contraction of stellar atmosphere. Cepheids and Delta Scuti stars exhibit harmonic sine curves.

Continuous Modulation
Noise / Glitch Type D
Aperiodic Scatter

Instrumental Artifacts

Spacecraft thermal drift, momentum wheel desaturations, cosmic ray strikes, and scattered Earthshine masquerading as physical dips.

Astrophysical Impostors

WHY BINARY STARS FOOL TRANSIT SEARCHES

Many stars do not live alone; they orbit a companion star. When two stars orbit edge-on, grazing eclipses create light curve dips that closely mimic planetary transits.

1
Phase 0.0 • Primary Eclipse The cooler, dimmer star occults the hotter star, causing a massive, sharp decrease in total luminous flux.
2
Phase 0.5 • Secondary Eclipse The hotter star occults the cooler companion half an orbit later. Planets never produce a visible secondary transit dip of this magnitude.
Epistemic Transparency

HONEST SCIENCE: HANDLING AMBIGUITY

Real telescope data is noisy. Rather than forcing a yes or no, the classifier gives calibrated probabilities, and anything without a class above 50% is kept for review.
