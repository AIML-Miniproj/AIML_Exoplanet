Stellar Classification 🪐 M_p / R_p

Exoplanet

Any celestial body of planetary mass orbiting a star, stellar remnant, or brown dwarf outside our solar system. Over 5,500 have been confirmed via space-based telescopes.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) classify candidates by matching transit profiles against physical boundary limits (mass < 13 Jupiter masses).

Photometry ΔF / F₀

Transit Method

Photometric technique measuring the miniscule periodic drop in a star’s brightness as an exoplanet crosses directly between the star and the observer's line of sight.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Primary detection pipeline input. Algorithms analyze duration, ingress slope, and duty cycle to compute orbital inclination and semi-major axis.

Photometry F(t) vs t

Light Curve

A time-series telemetry graph displaying light intensity received from a stellar target over standard observing cadences (e.g. 20-second or 2-minute cadence on TESS).

Why it matters in AI vetting

Raw time series are detrended using Gaussian Processes, Splines, or biweight filters prior to feeding recurrent neural network encoders.

Mission Specs HEO 2:1 Res

NASA TESS

The Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite. An all-sky space observatory scanning 24°×96° sectors every 27.4 days using four wide-field optical cameras.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Its larger 21-arcsecond pixels increase blending from nearby background stars, necessitating specialized centroid-shift AI models.

Photometry e⁻ / s

Flux (PDCSAP)

Pre-search Data Conditioning Simple Aperture Photometry. Normalized stellar photon count after instrument noise and systematic spacecraft jitter corrections.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Clean PDCSAP flux guarantees that deep neural networks learn astrophysical morphology rather than momentum dump thermal artifacts.

Orbital Dynamics P [days]

Orbital Period (P)

The time required for an exoplanet to complete one full revolution around its host star, tied directly to orbital semi-major axis by Kepler's Third Law.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Automated pipelines require at least 3 detected transits to establish periodicity and eliminate single-event spurious cosmic ray impacts.

AI & Statistics φ = (t - T₀)/P mod 1

Phase Folding

The mathematical folding of a continuous lightcurve modulo its discovered period, superimposing all recurring transit dips into a single composite cycle.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Increases the signal-to-noise ratio by √N (where N is transit count), providing clear 1D arrays for input into multi-layer perceptrons.

Photometry δ = (R_p / R_*)²

Transit Depth (δ)

The fraction of starlight blocked at mid-transit, typically measured in parts-per-million (ppm) or percentage, revealing the planetary radius ratio.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Depths exceeding 3% (~30,000 ppm) usually disqualify planet status for main sequence stars, flagging binary star stellar companions.

AI & Statistics TLS / BLS Power

Periodogram (BLS / TLS)

Box Least Squares and Transit Least Squares algorithms that fit inverted step or limb-darkened shapes across frequency spaces to locate hidden periodicities.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Signal Detection Efficiency (SDE) peaks from TLS serve as prime features in modern Random Forest ranking pipelines.

Stellar Types EB System

Eclipsing Binary (EB)

A system of two stars orbiting a mutual center of mass, whose orbital plane lies along our line of sight, creating alternating stellar eclipses.

Why it matters in AI vetting

The primary astrophysical mimic. Machine learning must detect subtle secondary eclipses or odd-even depth discrepancies.

Stellar Types Δmag(t)

Variable Star

Stars whose brightness fluctuates due to intrinsic physical changes: radial pulsations (Cepheids, RR Lyrae), surface flares, or rotation of large starspots.

Why it matters in AI vetting

High-frequency stellar flares can mimic steep transit egresses; automated wavelets remove stellar activity before planet classification.

AI & Statistics ΔCentroid

False Positive (NEBs)

Nearby Eclipsing Binaries: background stellar systems whose large eclipses are heavily diluted by the target star's flux, mimicking a tiny planetary transit.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Centroid pixel shift verification computes whether the center-of-light shifts during transit toward an adjacent pixel, exposing dilution.

Astrobiology T_eq [K]

Equilibrium Temp (T_eq)

The theoretical temperature reached by a planet modeled as a blackbody heated purely by its host star's incident flux, assuming Bond albedo redistribution.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Determines if the discovered body resides in the circumstellar Habitable Zone (200K – 320K) to prioritize James Webb (JWST) follow-up missions.

Orbital Dynamics r_L ≈ 0.49q^(2/3)

Roche Lobe

The teardrop-shaped gravitational boundary around a star in a binary system within which orbiting material is gravitationally bound to that star.

Why it matters in AI vetting

Overflow causes non-spherical distortion and mass transfer streams, manifesting as pronounced out-of-transit phase modulations.
